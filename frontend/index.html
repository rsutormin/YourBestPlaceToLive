How the map score works

Each metric is scored by comparing a country with the others. A typical country lands in the middle. Better results move the score up, toward green, and worse results move it down, toward red. When a lower number is better, such as cost or crime, the score is flipped so that better still means higher.

The sliders on Your Best List set how much each metric counts. They step through Not important, Slightly important, Important, Very important, and Extremely important. Not important leaves that metric out.

A country's overall score is the average of those metric scores, weighted by the sliders. A metric with no number for that country is left out of its average. Countries with numbers for fewer than about two thirds of the metrics stay uncolored.

The map paints the overall score from red, a weaker match for what you marked as important, through yellow to green, a stronger match.
